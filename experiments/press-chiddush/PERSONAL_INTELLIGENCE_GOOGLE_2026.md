## What the article establishes (reported)

1. **Mechanism — "Context Packing":** at query time Gemini decides whether
   personal context helps, then fetches live from connected apps (Gmail,
   Calendar, Drive/Workspace files, Photos, YouTube history, search history).
   No pre-indexed static profile; retrieval is per-query. Each app opts in
   separately. Works on free-tier models; personal accounts only (NOT Google
   Workspace); US-only at time of writing.
2. **Memory taxonomy:** ChatGPT memory = *declarative* (retains what you tell
   it); Personal Intelligence = *observational* (reconstructs you from your
   traces, cross-app). The article calls this the fundamental difference.
3. **The substrate law (the article's core):** answer quality is a function of
   ecosystem hygiene, not model quality. Same data, different structure →
   "résultat radicalement différent" (the two-Gmail rénovation test; the
   Sophie-homonym confusion; the "Brief V3 final" doc invisible because its
   title named neither client nor contact).
4. **Measured (by the journalist) before/after:** 200-doc Drive, 10 transversal
   queries: 4/10 found before restructuring → 8/10 after (explicit dated
   titles, `## Contexte / ## Décisions / ## Actions / ## Prochaines étapes`
   headers, Drive "Description" metadata filled). One afternoon of work.
5. **The residual 2/10:** queries about implicit information — a decision
   taken « entre les lignes », never explicitly written — fail EVEN AFTER
   restructuring. Verbatim law: *"Personal Intelligence a besoin que
   l'information soit énoncée quelque part. Il ne lit pas entre les lignes."*
6. **Working habits that move results:** 5–6 Gmail labels suffice (value is
   cleaner threads, not label-filtering); one thread = one subject (drifted
   threads confuse contexts); post-meeting synthesis mails — even to oneself —
   because *"un accord verbal dont il n'existe aucune trace écrite est
   invisible pour le modèle"*; titles carry decisions and dates.
7. **Query patterns:** specific on subject + vague on source wins ("qu'est-ce
   que Martin a proposé pour le planning ?" beats "cherche dans Gmail…");
   defined-objective queries beat broad ones ("mes sujets en attente de
   réponse cette semaine" beats "résume ma semaine"). Photos adds a register:
   OCR of labels/whiteboards, geolocated retrieval.
