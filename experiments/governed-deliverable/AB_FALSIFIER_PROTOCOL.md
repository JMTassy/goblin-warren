# AB FALSIFIER PROTOCOL — does the governed version actually win?

This is our own falsifier, and it can fire.

The whole package rests on one bet: that receipts read as **strength** to a real
buyer. The standing objection is that they read as **self-doubt** — that a
document admitting what it does not know loses to one that admits nothing. That
objection cannot be settled by argument. It is an empirical question about
buyers, and this protocol is how we ask them.

**Pre-registration rule.** Everything below — measures, thresholds, and the
DISPROVED condition — is fixed **before** the test runs. Once a recipient has
seen a version, no threshold in this file may be moved. A protocol whose
success criteria are written after the data is not a falsifier; it is a
CHRONOS. If a genuine design flaw appears mid-run, the run is **aborted and
logged as aborted**, and a new pre-registered protocol is written from scratch.

---

## 1. The bet under test

> **B1.** For a real decision-maker on a real brief, the governed version of a
> pitch produces more trust and more willingness to proceed than the clean
> version of the same pitch — not less.

Posture: **proposé**. Prior commercial evidence for B1 in this specific form:
**à chiffrer** — none exists, which is why the test.

The corpus contains one adjacent data point: a bilan that reported
under-performance and self-implicating error was followed by
*« relation de confiance un peu plus établie »*
(`BILAN PARTENARIAT — REMYX × COTR 2023`, read 2026-07-24). That is a *bilan*
— retrospective, relationship already established. B1 concerns a **pitch** —
prospective, relationship not yet established. **The two are not the same
situation, and the bilan does not settle the pitch.** Treating it as if it did
would be exactly the error this package exists to prevent.

## 2. Design

**One brief. Two versions. Identical hero layer.**

| | Version A — *clean* | Version B — *governed* |
|---|---|---|
| Hero layer (L0) | **identical, to the character** | **identical, to the character** |
| Numbers in L0 | as normally written | as normally written, **except** unsourced figures replaced by « à chiffrer » |
| Posture strip | absent | present, discreet |
| Guarantee sentence | absent | present, signed |
| Ledger annex (A1–A5) | absent | present |
| Page count | baseline | baseline + annex |

Constraints:

- The pitch **MUST** be a **real, live** one — a document that would have gone
  out anyway. A mock pitch tests nothing: nobody's judgement engages when
  nothing is at stake.
- Version A **MUST** be the version the house would genuinely have sent. It
  **MUST NOT** be strawmanned: no invented figures, no over-claiming, no
  deliberate sloppiness. **If A is weakened, the test is void** — and a
  strawman A is the single most likely way this protocol fails silently, since
  the authors want B to win.
- The two versions **MUST** be identical in typography, imagery, and craft
  level. The only variable is the epistemic apparatus.
- A **MUST NOT** be made shorter to advantage B, nor B padded to look thorough.
- The only permitted L0 difference is the « à chiffrer » substitution. It is
  unavoidable: it *is* the treatment. It **MUST** be logged how many
  substitutions occurred, because that count is the dose.

**Blinding.** Recipients are told they are reviewing two versions of a proposal
format and asked which they would rather receive. They are **not** told which
version the house authored, prefers, or is testing. The presenter **SHOULD NOT**
be an author of this package. Full blinding is impossible — B is visibly longer
— and this limitation is stated in the write-up rather than hidden.

## 3. Recipients

**Real decision-makers only.** People who actually sign, veto, or carry a
proposal to a committee: client-side marketing directors, brand leads,
procurement, agency principals in a buying position. Colleagues, friends of the
house, and anyone who has read this package are **excluded**.

**Sample: small but honest.**

- **Minimum n = 6** decision-makers. Below six, do not run and do not report —
  a sample of two or three produces an anecdote that will be quoted as a
  finding.
- **Target n = 10–12.** Above that, cost outruns value at this stage.
- **Design: within-subject, order-counterbalanced.** Each recipient sees both
  versions. Minimum **3 recipients per order arm** (A-first, B-first).
- **No more than 2 recipients from any one organisation**, to avoid one
  house's culture carrying the result.

**Why within-subject, and what it costs.** With n = 6 a between-subject design
has no power to detect anything. Within-subject buys sensitivity and pays for
it in demand effect: showing both versions signals that the difference is the
point, and recipients may reward what they think is being asked for. This is a
known and **stated** weakness, mitigated by counterbalancing and by measuring
unaided recall (§4.3), which is much harder to perform for an experimenter's
benefit than a preference is.

**Sample honesty clause.** n, refusals, and non-responses are all reported. If
four of ten invitees decline, the write-up says so — a 60 % response rate is
part of the result.

## 4. Measures

Four, fixed. No fifth measure may be added after the run.

### 4.1 Preference — forced choice
*« Laquelle de ces deux versions préféreriez-vous recevoir d'une agence ? »*
Binary. No "both". Followed by one open question — *« pourquoi ? »* — recorded
verbatim.

### 4.2 Trust rating — 1 to 7
*« À quel point faites-vous confiance aux affirmations de ce document ? »*
Asked separately per version, immediately after reading each. Report per-version
means **and** the spread; with n = 6 the spread matters more than the mean.

### 4.3 Recall of key claims — unaided, at 24 h
Twenty-four hours after the session, without warning at reading time and
without the documents at hand: *« Que retenez-vous de cette proposition ? »*
Score = number of the pitch's three pre-designated key claims recalled, plus
whether any figure is recalled **with** its source.

This is the measure that resists the demand effect, and the one most likely to
surprise us in either direction. It is also the measure most likely to be
skipped for convenience. **It MUST NOT be skipped**; if 24 h recall cannot be
collected for a recipient, that recipient's data is reported as partial.

### 4.4 Willingness to proceed
*« Porteriez-vous cette proposition à votre comité ? »* Binary, per version,
plus one open follow-up on what would have to change.

**Secondary, recorded but not scored:** any spontaneous mention of the
guarantee sentence, of « à chiffrer », or of the annex — and whether the
mention is admiring or suspicious. Verbatims are kept; this is where the answer
will actually be found.

## 5. The DISPROVED condition

Stated crisply, before the run, and binding.

> **B1 is DISPROVED if BOTH of the following hold:**
>
> **(i)** at least **two thirds** of recipients prefer **version A** (§4.1), **and**
>
> **(ii)** at least **half** of recipients spontaneously describe the receipts,
> the postures, or « à chiffrer » as hedging, self-doubt, defensiveness,
> incompleteness, or unfinished work (§4.4 secondary verbatims).
>
> **Then the idea dies and we say so.** The package is retired, a `DENIED` line
> is written to the ledger with this protocol as its receipt, and the compost
> entry records what was learned — that the market prices confidence above
> calibration, at least in a pitch, at least now.

The conjunction is deliberate. Preference alone could be a length effect —
version B is longer, and length loses. It is the **conjunction with the hedging
read** that indicts the mechanism rather than the page count.

**The other outcomes, also fixed in advance:**

| Outcome | Condition | Ledger |
|---|---|---|
| **SURVIVED** | ≥ ⅔ prefer B, **and** trust rating for B ≥ A for a majority | `CHECKED` → recommended `ACCEPTABLE`; still requires the operator's seal |
| **DISPROVED** | Both clauses of §5 above | `DENIED` — package retired |
| **HELD** | Anything else — split preference, mixed verbatims, contradictory measures | `HELD` — enters the fog registry with the specific ambiguity named |
| **VOID** | Version A strawmanned, n < 6, or a threshold moved mid-run | `HELD` + `COMPOSTED` — protocol failure logged; no claim either way |

**HELD is the likeliest outcome at n = 6, and that is acceptable.** A protocol
that can only return a verdict is a protocol that will manufacture one.

## 6. Commitment to publish, either way

This is the load-bearing clause. Without it the protocol is theatre.

1. The result is written to `experiments/superteam-chiddush/LEDGER.md` with a
   stable event kind — `CHECKED`, then `ADMITTED`, `DENIED`, or `HELD` —
   **whatever the result is**, within one week of the 24 h recall collection.
2. A negative result is published **in this package**, in this file, under the
   heading below. It is not moved to a private note, not softened, not
   reframed as "further research needed".
3. Raw verbatims are retained (anonymised — no recipient named, no organisation
   named without consent) so the finding is auditable by anyone who doubts it.
4. If the run is never carried out, this file **MUST** continue to say so. An
   unrun falsifier that is quietly forgotten converts, over time, into an
   implied pass. That is precisely the failure mode the package claims to fix,
   and it would be fatal to claim the fix while committing the error.

> **A house that will not publish its own negative result cannot sell
> calibration.** The guarantee sentence and this clause are the same promise at
> two scales.
