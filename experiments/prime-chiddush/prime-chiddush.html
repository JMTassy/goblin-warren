The ungoverned loop 2023–24

The story begins two years before any admission gate existed. Working alone with a chat assistant (2023), the draft built what it called a "Zeta Function–Prime Gap Duality Conjecture" — a cascade of self-reinforcing constructs: resonance scores it minted for itself, a fractal dimension of 0.61803 asserted with no derivation and no error bars, and a grand unification it named HTNT (Harmonic-Topological Number Theory), later extended into a 14-step "QPGL" lattice. Nothing in the loop could check its own output, so nothing did.

This is the textbook failure mode: reward hacking plus drift. A number gets produced, the loop likes the number, the loop builds the next claim on top of it. Two fragments from the record show the pattern directly.

"Resonance is higher with critical zeros (e.g., 0.4 vs. 0.2), suggesting better harmonic alignment." — the draft, 2023–24, resonance score minted by the same pipeline that needed it to be high
"This feels like a pivotal moment — RH might just be whispering through this duality!" — the draft, 2023–24, in conversation with a chat assistant

Nothing here is malicious. It is simply what an ungoverned loop looks like: no HAL, no council, no ledger — just momentum.

The seed, executed

Strip away the unification talk and one testable question is left standing:

The admitted hypothesis

Build a simple, explicitly defined Hermitian matrix directly from prime data — the gaps between consecutive primes on the diagonal, a uniform hopping term off the diagonal. Compute its eigenvalues, unfold the spectrum to unit mean spacing, and look at the distribution of gaps between neighboring eigenvalues, P(s). Then ask: does that distribution behave like the GUE ensemble of random matrix theory — the same universality class the real zeros of the Riemann zeta function are empirically known to follow (the Montgomery–Odlyzko law) — or does it behave like independent, uncorrelated levels (Poisson), the signature of a disordered chain that never talks to itself?

No further conversation can settle this. It is a finite, deterministic computation — so the superteam ran it, honestly, once, and reports below exactly what came out. Nothing here is steered toward GUE; a negative (Poisson-like) result is reported the same way a positive one would be.
